💳 3. Account Data: Cardholder Data (CHD) vs Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD)

Account data is bifurcated into Cardholder Data (CHD) and Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD). Storing SAD post-authorization is the single most penalized violation in payment security!

Category Data Element Store Post-Auth? Mandatory Protection Standard
Cardholder Data (CHD) Primary Account Number (PAN) YES Must be rendered unreadable anywhere it is stored: 1) Strong cryptography (AES-128/256), 2) Keyed hash, 3) Truncation, or 4) Tokenization.
Cardholder Data (CHD) Cardholder Name YES Must be protected in accordance with PCI DSS if stored together with the PAN.
Cardholder Data (CHD) Expiration Date YES Must be protected in accordance with PCI DSS if stored together with the PAN.
Cardholder Data (CHD) Service Code (3-digit magnetic stripe code) YES Must be protected in accordance with PCI DSS if stored together with the PAN.
Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD) Full Magnetic Stripe Data (Track 1 / Track 2 / Chip equivalent) NO NEVER CANNOT BE STORED POST-AUTHORIZATION under any circumstances, even if encrypted. Only Issuers with business justification may retain SAD.
Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD) Card Verification Code (CAV2, CVC2, CVV2, CID) NO NEVER CANNOT BE STORED POST-AUTHORIZATION. Storing CVV for recurring billing is strictly illegal under PCI DSS!
Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD) PIN and Encrypted PIN Block NO NEVER CANNOT BE STORED POST-AUTHORIZATION. Plaintext PINs must never exist outside certified PTS cryptographic hardware.

🔑 Key Cryptographic Rules to Remember:

  • Display Masking (Req 3.3): Maximum display on screens/receipts is the first 6 (or 8) and last 4 digits (BIN + last 4). Full PAN requires documented business justification.
  • Rendering Stored PAN Unreadable (Req 3.5): 4 methods only: 1) One-way keyed hash, 2) Truncation, 3) Tokenization, 4) Strong cryptography (AES-128/256). Base64 and ROT13 are NOT encryption.
  • Dual Control & Split Knowledge (Req 3.6): Key management operations require 2 people (dual control) and key components divided so no single person possesses the full key (split knowledge).
  • DEK vs KEK: Data Encryption Key (DEK) encrypts card data; Key Encrypting Key (KEK) encrypts the DEK. KEK must be at least as cryptographically strong as the DEK and stored separately.

🏛️ 4. Payment Ecosystem Roles & Governance

PCI SSC vs Card Brands & Acquirers

  • • PCI SSC (Council): Founded by Visa, MC, Amex, Discover, JCB. Creates, publishes, and maintains standards (DSS, P2PE, SSF, PTS) and qualifies assessors (QSA, ISA, PCIP, ASV). THE COUNCIL DOES NOT ENFORCE COMPLIANCE AND LEVIES NO FINES!
  • • Payment Card Brands: Enforce compliance, establish merchant/provider levels (Level 1-4), set submission deadlines, and assess penalties against Acquiring Banks for merchant non-compliance.
  • • Acquirer (Merchant's Bank): Financial institution that contracts with merchants to process cards and disburse settled funds. Acquirers pass brand penalties down to non-compliant merchants.
  • • Issuer (Cardholder's Bank): Bank that issues cards to consumers. ONLY THE ISSUER has the authority to approve or decline a transaction during authorization.

Transaction Lifecycle (The ACS Cycle)

  • 1. Authorization: Merchant requests payment approval; Issuer validates cardholder funds/credit and returns approval or decline code.
  • 2. Clearing: Acquirer and Issuer exchange final settlement records via the Brand payment network.
  • 3. Settlement: Movement of funds from Issuer to Acquirer, and Acquirer deposits funds into the Merchant bank account.
  • • Open-Loop (4-Party): Visa & MasterCard. Separate Issuers and Acquirers participate.
  • • Closed-Loop (3-Party): American Express & Discover. Brand acts as both Issuer and Acquirer directly.
  • • Brand Programs: Visa = CISP; MasterCard = SDP; Discover = DISC; Amex = DSOP.

Assessor Qualifications & Code of Responsibility:

• QSA: Independent consultant who audits Level 1 entities and signs Reports on Compliance (ROC).
• ISA: Internal employee trained by PCI SSC to sponsor internal compliance and perform internal assessments.
• PCIP: Individual credential demonstrating comprehensive knowledge of PCI standards; does not sign official audit ROCs.
• ASV: Qualified vendor certified to perform quarterly external vulnerability scans.
• Code of Responsibility Sanctions: 1) Warning, 2) Suspension, 3) Revocation. Conflict of interest must be disclosed in writing immediately.

🎯 5. Scoping & Network Segmentation

1. Cardholder Data Environment (CDE)

Systems, people, and processes that store, process, or transmit Cardholder Data (CHD) or Sensitive Authentication Data (SAD), plus any system on the same unsegmented network segment.

2. Connected-To Systems

Systems that connect directly to the CDE (e.g. administrator jump boxes, administrative workstations, backup servers, SIEM collectors). Fully in scope for all applicable requirements.

3. Security-Impacting Systems

Systems that provide security functions or whose compromise could impact CDE security (e.g., Active Directory / LDAP, NTP time servers, DNS servers, patch management systems, firewalls).

⚡ Critical Scoping Rules:

  • Flat Network Danger: If an organization has no internal firewalls or VLAN segmentation, the entire corporate network (including HR and cafeteria PCs) is in scope for all 12 requirements!
  • Segmentation Testing: Network segmentation is not a requirement, but a strongly recommended method to reduce scope. Segmentation controls MUST be validated via technical penetration testing: annually for merchants, semi-annually (every 6 months) for service providers.
  • Out-of-Scope Systems: To be considered out-of-scope, a system component must have zero physical or logical access to the CDE, cannot communicate with CDE systems on any port, and must not impact CDE security.

📋 6. The 12 Requirements & 6 Overarching Goals

Goal 1: Build and Maintain a Secure Network and Systems

• Req 1: Install and Maintain Network Security Controls (NSCs): Restrict incoming and outgoing traffic to explicitly authorized business needs (default deny all). Review rulesets every 6 months. Direct connections between Internet and CDE strictly prohibited (DMZ required).

• Req 2: Apply Secure Configurations to All System Components: Change all vendor-supplied defaults (passwords, SNMP strings) prior to deployment. Maintain documented hardening standards (CIS/NIST). One primary function per system/VM. Encrypt non-console admin access (SSH/HTTPS; Telnet/HTTP forbidden).

Goal 2: Protect Account Data

• Req 3: Protect Stored Account Data: SAD (track, CVV, PIN) cannot be stored post-authorization. Render PAN unreadable using strong crypto, keyed hash, truncation, or tokenization. Mask PAN on displays (max first 6/8 and last 4). Key management dual control & split knowledge.

• Req 4: Protect Cardholder Data with Strong Cryptography During Transmission: Use strong cryptography (TLS 1.2/1.3) when transmitting PAN over open, public networks (Internet, cellular, wireless). Insecure ciphers (RC4, 3DES, SSL) prohibited. Never send cleartext PAN via email/SMS/chat.

Goal 3: Maintain a Vulnerability Management Program

• Req 5: Protect All Systems and Networks from Malicious Software: Deploy anti-malware on all susceptible systems. Document Targeted Risk Analysis (TRA) for non-susceptible systems. Automatic updates and behavioral/heuristic detection required. Deploy automated anti-phishing technical mechanisms (DMARC/SPF/DKIM).

• Req 6: Develop and Maintain Secure Systems and Software: Deploy Critical/High security patches within 30 calendar days. Protect public web applications with WAF. Inventory and verify authorization/integrity for all browser scripts on payment pages (Req 6.4.3). Independent code reviews and separate dev/test/production environments (no live PAN in test!).

Goal 4: Implement Strong Access Control Measures

• Req 7: Restrict Access to System Components and Cardholder Data by Business Need to Know: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) enforcing least privilege. Review all user accounts and privileges at least every 6 months (Req 7.2.4).

• Req 8: Identify Users and Authenticate Access to System Components: Unique user ID for every individual (shared accounts banned). Minimum 12-character passwords (v4.0). Lockout after <= 10 failed attempts for >= 30 minutes. Inactive accounts disabled within 90 days. 15-minute idle screen lock. MFA mandatory for ALL access entering the CDE (Req 8.4.2).

• Req 9: Restrict Physical Access to Cardholder Data: Physical badges and escorts for visitors. Retain visitor logs and video camera footage for at least 90 days. Periodic physical inspection of POI payment devices for skimming (Req 9.5). Cross-cut shred paper media; secure wipe/degauss/shred electronic media.

Goal 5: Regularly Monitor and Test Networks

• Req 10: Log and Monitor All Access to System Components and Cardholder Data: Audit log details: User, Event, Time, Status, Origin, Affected asset. Daily log reviews of critical security systems via automated SIEM. NTP time synchronization across all systems. Retain logs for 1 year total (3 months immediately accessible).

• Req 11: Test Security of Systems and Networks Regularly: Quarterly rogue wireless scans. Quarterly internal vulnerability scans (authenticated). Quarterly external scans by ASV (zero CVSS >= 4.0). Annual internal/external penetration testing (and after significant changes). Weekly FIM checks. Weekly payment page tamper checks (Req 11.6.1).

Goal 6: Maintain an Information Security Policy

• Req 12: Support Information Security with Organizational Policies and Programs: Review security policy annually. Annual risk assessment. Annual scope confirmation (semi-annually for service providers). Annual security awareness training with phishing drills. TPSP written agreements and annual compliance status monitoring (AOCs). Test Incident Response Plan annually.

🧭 7. SAQ Selection Flowchart & Criteria

SAQ Type Eligibility Requirements & Processing Channel Payment Architecture
SAQ A Card-not-present (e-commerce or mail/phone) merchants who 100% outsource all payment processing to a validated PCI DSS compliant third party. No electronic cardholder data storage. Hosted iFrame or URL Redirect
SAQ A-EP E-commerce merchants who host the checkout website that delivers payment scripts directing card data from the consumer browser to the payment processor. Merchant servers impact transaction security. Direct Post, JavaScript API, Custom Checkout
SAQ B Brick-and-mortar merchants using standalone, dial-up payment terminals connected via traditional analog telephone lines; no electronic cardholder data storage. Dial-up Terminal (POTS analog line)
SAQ B-IP Merchants using standalone PTS-approved point-of-interaction (POI) payment terminals connected via IP network (Ethernet/Wi-Fi); no electronic card storage. Standalone IP-connected PTS Terminals
SAQ C-VT Merchants processing card transactions manually one-by-one via an online Virtual Terminal provided by a compliant processor, on an isolated computer with no card storage. Web Browser Virtual Terminal
SAQ C Merchants with payment application systems (e.g. POS software on PC) connected to the Internet, with no electronic cardholder data storage. POS Software connected to Internet
SAQ P2PE Merchants using a PCI SSC-listed, validated Point-to-Point Encryption (P2PE) hardware solution with PTS POI terminals; no electronic card storage. Validated Hardware P2PE (~33 requirements)
SAQ D Merchant All other merchants that do not qualify for SAQs A through P2PE, including ANY merchant that stores cardholder data electronically. Full Merchant DSS (All Reqs)
SAQ D Service Provider ALL eligible Service Providers. Service providers are NEVER eligible for SAQ A, B, or C under any circumstances! Sole SAQ for Service Providers

📚 8. Appendices Deep-Dive (A1, A2, A3, C, D)

Appendix A: Supplemental Validation

  • • Appendix A1 (Multi-Tenant Service Providers): Applies to cloud/hosting providers. Mandates logical tenant isolation, hypervisor security, preventing cross-tenant access to data/logs, and customer penetration test rights.
  • • Appendix A2 (Shared Hosting Providers): Legacy shared web hosting providers must protect cardholder data and maintain isolated audit logging for each merchant.
  • • Appendix A3 (DESV): Designated Entities Supplemental Validation. Mandated by card brands or acquirers for entities with high breach history or systemic non-compliance. Requires formal continuous BAU compliance programs and an executive compliance officer.

Appendix C vs Appendix D

  • • Appendix C (Compensating Controls): Used when a technical or business constraint prevents meeting the Defined Requirement. Financial cost or lack of budget is NEVER accepted as a constraint! Must meet 4 tests: 1) intent/rigor, 2) offset risk, 3) above and beyond other requirements, 4) commensurate with risk. Documented on Compensating Controls Worksheet (CCW).
  • • Appendix D (Customized Approach): Flexible option in v4.0 for mature entities to meet the Requirement's Stated Objective using custom controls. Requires NO constraint! Mandates a documented Targeted Risk Analysis (TRA) for every custom requirement, and the Assessor designs custom audit test procedures.

🛡️ 9. Specialized PCI Standards Quick Reference

PCI P2PE

Point-to-Point Encryption

Hardware-to-hardware encryption from a certified PTS terminal directly to the provider's secure decryption environment. Merchant systems never see plaintext PAN, drastically reducing scope to SAQ P2PE.

PCI PTS POI

PIN Transaction Security

Hardware terminal security standard. Evaluates physical and logical tamper-resistance, cryptographic key protection, and anti-skimming controls for card readers and PIN pads.

PCI SSF

Software Security Framework

Replaced PA-DSS. Comprises: 1) Secure Software Standard (payment software technical security) and 2) Secure Software Lifecycle (Secure SLC) Standard (vendor software development processes).

PCI 3DS Core

3-D Secure Standard

Cardholder identity authentication protocol for Card-Not-Present e-commerce transactions, enabling cardholders to authenticate with their card issuer prior to purchase authorization.

PCI PIN

PIN Security Standard

Governs the secure management, processing, and transmission of Personal Identification Numbers (PINs) at ATMs and unattended/attended POS terminals.

PCI MPoC

Mobile Payments on COTS

Standard for accepting contactless payments (Tap to Pay) and PIN entry directly on commercial off-the-shelf consumer devices (smartphones and tablets).

⚠️ 10. Top 25 Cardinal Exam Traps & Rules

1. SAD Post-Auth: NEVER allowed post-auth under ANY circumstances, even if customer signs consent or data is encrypted! Only Issuers have an exception.
2. Council Enforcement: The PCI SSC has ZERO enforcement power and assesses NO fines. Only Card Brands and Acquirers enforce compliance.
3. Critical Patching: Must be deployed within 30 calendar days (1 month). Not 24 hours, not 90 days.
4. User Access Reviews: User accounts and privileges must be reviewed at least once every 6 months (semi-annually).
5. Password Length: Baseline in v4.0 is 12 characters (alphanumeric), or 8 if system technical constraints are documented.
6. Universal CDE MFA: Required for ALL users entering the CDE (Req 8.4.2), not just remote users or administrators.
7. Account Lockout: Max 10 failed login attempts; lockout duration minimum 30 minutes.
8. Inactive Accounts: Disabled or deleted within 90 days of inactivity.
9. Physical Logs & CCTV: Visitor access logs and video surveillance footage must be retained for at least 90 calendar days.
10. Audit Log Retention: Retain logs for 1 year total, with the most recent 3 months immediately accessible online.
11. Daily Log Review: Required daily for critical security systems (NSCs, IDS, auth servers) and servers storing/processing CHD.
12. File Integrity Monitoring: FIM must check critical files at least weekly (every 7 days).
13. Rogue Wireless Scans: Quarterly testing is mandatory even if company policy prohibits wireless networks!
14. External ASV Scans: Must be performed quarterly by an ASV. Zero vulnerabilities with CVSS >= 4.0 permitted to pass.
15. Pentest Frequency: At least annually (12 months) and after any significant change.
16. Service Provider Segmentation: Service providers must test segmentation controls every 6 months (merchants test annually).
17. Service Provider Scope: Service providers must confirm scope every 6 months (merchants confirm annually).
18. Service Provider SAQ: Service providers can NEVER use SAQ A, B, or C. They must use SAQ D for Service Providers.
19. Card Data Storage: ANY electronic card storage immediately disqualifies a merchant from SAQs A, B, B-IP, C-VT, C, P2PE and mandates SAQ D.
20. Virtual Terminal (SAQ C-VT): Manual entry of single card transactions into a browser web portal provided by processor = SAQ C-VT.
21. Compensating Controls & Cost: Budget shortfalls or financial cost are NEVER acceptable justifications for compensating controls!
22. Customized Approach & TRA: Does not require a constraint, but requires a documented Targeted Risk Analysis (TRA) for every custom control.
23. Payment Page Scripts (Req 6.4.3): Inventory, business justification, and integrity check for ALL scripts executing in browser on payment pages.
24. Live PAN in Dev/Test: Strictly forbidden under Requirement 6.5.3 under all circumstances!
25. Transaction Approval: ONLY the Card Issuer approves or declines transactions during authorization.

💡 Golden Item-Writer Rule #1: Action Verb Clue

When a stem asks for controls to "detect and defend" against phishing (Req 5.4.1), item-writers deliberately plant "Annual employee awareness training" (Req 12.6.3) as a trap! Awareness training is an administrative control; 5.4.1 mandates automated technical mechanisms (anti-spoofing, SPF, DKIM, DMARC).

Example Exam Item Stem: Req 5.4.1 vs 12.6.3

"Under PCI DSS Requirement 5.4.1, what controls must an entity implement to detect and defend personnel against phishing attacks?"

❌ Trap A: Annual security awareness training detailing social engineering attack vectors (This is Req 12.6.3, not 5.4.1).
✅ Correct C: Automated technical mechanisms such as anti-spoofing protocols (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and email filtering.
The Tell-Tale Takeaway: Notice the verbs: "Detect and defend" = automated technology (filters/protocols). "Educate / train / maintain awareness" = personnel training (Req 12.6).
